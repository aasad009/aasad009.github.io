Evolutionary biomechanics · fossil function

Reconstructing the missing mechanics of vertebrate evolution.

Fossils preserve bones, but not the full muscles and mechanical conditions that enabled animals to move. Three linked research proposals ask what we can reliably reconstruct, which functional conclusions remain robust, and how vertebral organization changed across evolutionary history.

The researcher behind the questions

Connecting computational mechanics with evolutionary discovery.

I’m a mechanical engineering PhD candidate at UC Riverside. My work develops methods for inferring material behavior from limited evidence, quantifying uncertainty, and testing physical models. These proposals explore how such methods could complement specialist fossil anatomy and comparative biology to clarify the mechanical lives of extinct vertebrates.

Scientific scope: These are computational research proposals, not empirical fossil results. The interfaces use synthetic anatomies or reduced-order models to illustrate how preserved anatomy, extant calibration, and biomechanics could be combined in evolutionary research.
