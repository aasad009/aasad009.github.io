Comparative anatomy · biomechanics · evolutionary function

From vertebrate anatomy to movement and evolution.

Living vertebrates let us directly test how skeletal anatomy, muscles, joints, and spinal regions shape movement. Comparative studies then reveal which functional patterns extend across species and which can inform evolutionary interpretations, including extinct lineages. These three proposals begin with measurable biology and use uncertainty-aware mechanics to connect form with function.

FROM MY EXISTING RESEARCH TO THESE QUESTIONS

Three methods I have developed. Three ways to study vertebrate function.

My published and preprint results concern material characterization and inverse mechanics, not yet comparative vertebrate biomechanics. Their value here is in designing informative extant tests, keeping competing mechanical explanations visible, and estimating properties from observed deformation before investigating evolutionary transitions.

OPTIMAL EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN · PUBLISHED 2025

Which observation would actually resolve uncertainty?

Demonstrated For synthetic noisy hyperelastic tests, selecting informative loading states improved parameter-identification repeatability relative to less informative sampling.

Proposed use In muscle-anatomy inference and comparative joint function, choose informative living-species specimens, tissue tests and joint poses to distinguish competing anatomical and mechanical explanations.

CONSTITUTIVE-COMPATIBILITY ATLAS · PREPRINT 2026

What if several material laws explain the same evidence?

Demonstrated A noise-aware atlas keeps multiple hyperelastic model families compatible with limited measurements; an additional withheld deformation mode differentiated alternatives in a rubber benchmark.

Proposed use In joint-function reconstruction, propagate plausible sets of soft-tissue responses instead of assuming one tissue material law for every species. Test whether differences in joint mobility remain robust across species and evolutionary comparisons.

Asadi & Laksari · bioRxiv 2026 ↗
PHYSICS-CONSTRAINED 3D INVERSION · PUBLISHED 2026

Can deformation reveal spatially varying tissue properties?

Demonstrated Stress-constrained PI-UNet recovered heterogeneous nonlinear material-parameter fields from volumetric deformation in controlled synthetic finite-element benchmarks.

Proposed use With suitable measured deformation data from living species, extend inverse mechanics toward tissue-property priors for joint and spine comparisons. This would require new experimental validation and appropriate tissue laws.

Asadi & Laksari · Annals of Biomedical Engineering 2026 ↗

The scientific boundary: these methods were demonstrated in synthetic or benchmark mechanics studies, not yet in comparative vertebrate samples. New anatomical and mechanical measurements in living species are essential both as scientific endpoints and as a foundation for evolutionary inference.
