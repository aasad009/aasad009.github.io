Research direction 3 · Full research proposal

From Microstructural Evolution to Mechanical Evolution

Can established microanatomical and physiological histories be translated into otherwise unmeasured macroscopic constitutive histories?

Conditional hypothesis • Cross-scale calibration • Evolutionary reconstruction

Core premise

If a transferable, experimentally validated transformation exists between tissue microanatomy/physiology and macroscopic mechanical response, then independently known changes in microanatomy and physiology can be transported through that transformation to reconstruct macroscopic mechanical evolution.

The project does not assume that genes or environmental exposure directly specify an elastic modulus. They shape measurable biological states; those states are inputs to the mechanical transformation. Nor does it assume that the micro-to-macro relationship is unique, universal, or smooth. A set-valued or probabilistic transformation may be required.

Key conceptual distinction

We first learn the mapping from paired structural, physiological, and mechanical observations. We then freeze that mapping and apply it to independently documented time series or comparative reconstructions of microanatomy. The primary output is a trajectory through macroscopic constitutive-response space, not a single stiffness estimate and not initially a joint-mobility prediction.

Scientific gaps to address

Gap 1 | Cross-scale transfer validity. Multiscale constitutive models and homogenization already exist; the unresolved test is whether a microstructure-conditioned model can predict multiaxial, nonlinear, and time-dependent response fingerprints on genuinely held-out specimens, loading paths, and physiological states.

Gap 2 | Transport of observed biological histories. Adaptation, development, aging, and phylogenetic changes in tissue architecture are studied, but the ability to translate independently established microhistories into validated full-response mechanical trajectories is less established.

Gap 3 | Evolutionary uncertainty and nonuniqueness. Multiple microstates may generate equivalent macro responses; conversely, small physiological differences may produce large shifts. An atlas can quantify ambiguity and identify which evolutionary mechanical conclusions are robust.

Gap 4 | Genetic and environmental mechanisms. Genotype, ecology, and loading history may explain microanatomical trajectories. Their effects should be estimated with longitudinal, experimental, or phylogenetically controlled evidence, not inserted as arbitrary sliders in a constitutive model.

Mathematical statement

Let x(s) = [A(s), P(s)] denote microarchitecture A and physiological state P along a documented biological sequence s. Let H be a calibrated micro-to-macro operator and F a macromechanical response fingerprint over standardized loading protocols. The scientific claim is conditional:

F(s) ∈ H[x(s); protocol] , ΔF(s) = H[x(s)] − H[x(0)] , M(s) = J[G(s), F(s), loads] (optional downstream stage)

Here H may output a distribution or compatibility set. PCA is only a visualization of F(s); it is not itself the physical transform. Movement M(s) is assessed after the mechanical trajectory has been validated.

Research plan

Aim 1 — Establish and falsify the micro-to-macro transform

Begin with one tissue and a tractable range of states (initially trabecular bone or collagen-rich periarticular tissue, subject to available matched measurements). Assemble specimen-matched micro-CT/histology, physiological-state variables and mechanical tests. Construct physics-based RVE/homogenization predictions and compare against density-only, fixed-family and flexible statistical baselines. Predict held-out stresses over multiple deformation modes, not only one modulus. Report calibration, uncertainty coverage, representative-volume limitations and failure cases.

Go/no-go: H must outperform simpler baselines on specimen-held-out tests, preserve uncertainty coverage, and remain physically admissible. If this fails, mechanical evolution cannot yet be inferred from microhistories.

Aim 2 — Translate independently documented biological changes

Obtain an external longitudinal or reconstructed series x(s): development, controlled adaptation/remodeling, physiologic state change, or a phylogenetically supported anatomical reconstruction. Use the frozen H—without fitting to target-stage macro data—to calculate F(s) across the deformation-response atlas. Quantify changes in directional stiffness, nonlinear response, rate dependence, and material-model compatibility sets. Test whether predicted changes agree with any withheld mechanical measurements of later stages.

Go/no-go: Demonstrate prospective/held-out prediction of the direction and magnitude of a mechanical change, including uncertainty. A fitted trajectory from the same response data does not qualify as independent validation.

Aim 3 — Establish evolutionary interpretation and functional consequences

First compare within-species trajectories and population variability, distinguishing short-term physiological change from lifetime remodeling. Next test multiple taxa with adequate phylogenetic and allometric data, accounting for species ancestry and habitat. Only then couple the predicted tissue-response histories to the joint-mechanics manifold framework of Direction 1 to assess which changes in mechanical behavior alter mobility or locomotor function.

Go/no-go: Claims of inherited evolutionary change require comparative/phylogenetic support and successful held-out-species tests. A two-species contrast or simulated trajectory alone is insufficient.

How genes and environment enter

Genes and exposures condition the distribution of observed biological trajectories, p[x(s) | genotype, environment, loading, age, lineage]. They are explanatory variables for the evolution of x(s), not direct predictors of tissue stress unless this causal pathway is independently tested. For fossil taxa, anatomical reconstruction may be possible, but physiological/genetic states require explicitly uncertain extant-informed priors.

Risks and mitigation

Scale separation may fail; quantify representative-volume limits. Missing fluid or cellular state may make H nonstationary; stratify by physiological regime. Material families may be indistinguishable; report compatibility sets. Reconstructed ancestral states may be uncertain; propagate their distributions. Genetics/environment may be confounded with age, body size, or lifestyle; separate causal interventions from associations. Do not extrapolate across species without out-of-distribution tests.

Immediate study and demo relationship

The companion demo accepts a sequence of microanatomical/physiological states, maps them through a single illustrative fiber-reinforced transfer, and draws the resulting path on a PCA of macro-response fingerprints plus changing stress–stretch curves. All bundled trajectories are synthetic examples. The research objective is to replace those synthetic inputs and surrogate H with documented microhistories and a validated transform. A joint-movement readout is illustrative and secondary.
