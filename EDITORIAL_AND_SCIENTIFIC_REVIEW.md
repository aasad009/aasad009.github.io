# Final editorial and scientific review

## Editorial objective

The three proposal texts were reviewed paragraph-by-paragraph and rewritten in a direct, mechanics-focused research voice. Long reviewer-facing constructions were replaced with the explicit sequence: evolutionary question → testable hypothesis → experimental design → alternatives/nulls → interpretation. The tone aims to be clear and suitable for an introductory exchange with an evolutionary biologist. It does not claim to reproduce a private author's distinctive style exactly.

## What changed

- **Missing muscles:** Clear separation between published anatomical reconstruction and the proposed contribution: honest identification of recoverable soft-tissue properties; extant masked tests; phylogenetically held-out calibration.
- **Joint evolution:** Explicit distinction between passive motion, load-achievable motion, force-generating potential and observed behavior; the seven-family hyperelastic atlas is a baseline, not a validated animal-tissue library.
- **Spinal function:** Falsifiable order-invariance null under constant moment and independent serial joints; dependence on measured intact-column loading for physical validation.

There were **22 substantive paragraph/aim edits in each proposal (66 total)**. Reference lists and already-accurate headings were retained. The HTML beneath each corresponding demo was regenerated from the edited Word file and checked for paragraph-level equivalence.

## Publication checks and accurate scope

- Asadi & Laksari (2026), PI-UNet article: DOI `10.1007/s10439-026-04321-4`; synthetic 3D benchmarks, not validation on fossils.

## Scientific limits still applicable

1. None of the interactive demos uses measured fossil muscle tissue or experimentally validated extinct locomotion. They are pedagogical surrogates.
2. A conditional reconstruction set depends on sampled extant taxa, anatomical homology and fossil preservation. Interval coverage is an empirical test, not a given.
3. Bone observations do not uniquely identify tissue law, activation, pennation or maximum muscle force.
4. A passive pose envelope should not be interpreted as an animal's actual gait.
5. A mechanical change is not, by itself, evidence of historical natural selection or adaptation.
6. No lab datasets, specimens, collaboration agreements, or external experimental validation are secured by these files.
